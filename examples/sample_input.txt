The analyzer reads plain text and reports descriptive measurements. It counts words, sentences, and paragraphs without judging the writing in any way.

Short sentences vary the rhythm. Longer sentences, such as this one, carry explanations that need more room to develop before they can conclude; the mix of lengths is simply recorded as numbers.

Punctuation matters for style: commas separate clauses, colons introduce lists — apples, oranges, pears — and parentheses add asides (like this one). Questions appear rarely in reports? Exclamations almost never do!

The analyzer also records repeated terms. In this sample, the words "analyzer" and "sample" recur deliberately, so the N-gram inventory has something to find: the analyzer processes the sample, the sample exercises the analyzer, and the analyzer reports the sample. Technical terms such as JSON stay untouched because the analyzer never rewrites anything.

Finally, readability estimates summarize surface complexity. They describe the text; they do not grade the author, and they certainly do not guess who wrote it.
